1. Name and Identity Formatting
   • All names entered into the system must follow a specific standardized format.
   • Titles must be included for all individuals, such as Mr., Mrs., Dr., or Prof.
   • Initially, there was a suggestion to make direct name entry optional for some courses, but this was retracted.
   • All name entries are compulsory.

⸻

2. Staff Information Requirements
   • Staff records must include:
   • Gender
   • Surname
   • Middle name
   • First name
   • Highest academic qualification
   • Academic rank
   • Only the Head of Department (HOD) is permitted to make official submissions.
   • The Dean has commenting privileges only.

⸻

3. Course Table Adjustments
   • Remove lecturer name from the course table.
   • Replace it with:
   • Prerequisites, or
   • New lecturer assignment, where applicable.
   • Free-text or inconsistent entries should not be allowed.
   • All entries must follow programmatically enforced casing rules (no random capitalization).

⸻

4. Course Naming and Classification Rules
   • Course titles must be written in sentence case.
   • Courses must be categorized by type, for example:
   • General
   • Core
   • Other (for cross-program or external use)
   • Each course must also have an associated department level.

⸻

5. Cross-Department and Shared Courses
   • Some courses are created at the department level, while others are created at a school or program level.
   • Courses that originate in one department but are used by others should:
   • Be defined once at the source department.
   • Be referenced programmatically by other departments or programs.
   • These are not “general studies” courses, but core courses shared across multiple departments within the same school.

⸻

6. Course–Program Relationships
   • Courses can have:
   • One-to-one relationships with programs, or
   • One-to-many relationships with programs.
   • The system must clearly define and enforce these relationships.

⸻

7. Change Propagation and Governance
   • Any structural change made by the HOD—for example, moving a course from second semester to another—must:
   • Automatically reflect everywhere the course is referenced.
   • Update program structures and dependencies consistently.

1. Borrowed / Service Courses (Wide System Reflection)
   • This discussion applies specifically to borrowed or service courses.
   • Any change to such courses must reflect system-wide, not just locally.
   • The system must clearly represent dependencies:
   • Courses required for other courses
   • Courses that are general
   • Courses taken at lower levels that feed into higher-level courses
   • Courses that are core for some programs and requirements for others

⸻

2. Course Dependency and Ownership Models
   • Some courses are:
   • Created by one department but taken by students of other departments.
   • Not taken by students of the originating department, but exist solely for the benefit of others.
   • These courses must:
   • Clearly show the originating department
   • Be linked programmatically to dependent departments and schools
   • The system must avoid non-uniformity by:
   • Enforcing correct dependency structures
   • Testing relationships through proper linking
   • Workflow integrity is critical.

⸻

3. Hosting vs. Consuming Departments
   • Departments host courses for other departments and schools.
   • The hosting department’s students may not take those courses.
   • Other departments consume the course via references, not duplication.
   • All visibility and access must respect this structure.

⸻

4. GDS / Citizenship Orientation Courses
   • Question raised: Who owns and manages citizenship orientation courses?
   • Required relationship:
   • Between the GDS Office and the Service/Originating Department
   • Key clarifications:
   • GDS courses belong to departments
   • The GDS office does not generate courses
   • The office does not teach the courses
   • Their role is to coordinate and harmonize
   • GDS should only see:
   • Courses relevant to them
   • Courses linked back to their originating departments

⸻

5. Uniformity and Data Governance
   • Uniformity of entries is mandatory.
   • What is entered must be:
   • Standardized
   • Consistent
   • Programmatically enforced
   • Dependencies must be:
   • Validated
   • Tested
   • Correctly linked before approval

⸻

6. Roles and Permissions

Super Admins
• Have all system rights
• Full control over configuration and data

Admins
• Review submissions
• Accept or decline entries
• Regulate comments and corrections
• Can add courses and:
• Make them available to multiple departments
• Select all applicable departments at once

⸻

7. Submissions, Reviews, and Workflow
   • Departments submit entries.
   • Admins review and moderate.
   • Comments are regulated to ensure consistency.
   • Approved entries become part of official outputs.

⸻

8. Bulletin and Document Generation
   • The team can choose output formats:
   • Word
   • PDF
   • HTML
   • Documents can be generated:
   • For personal use
   • For a specific department
   • For an entire school
   • From the Academic Office perspective, three official documents can be generated:
   1. Department-level bulletin
   2. School-level bulletin
   3. General (institution-wide) bulletin
      • Bulletins can also be generated:
      • Department-by-department
      • School-by-school

10. Course Categories and Rules
    • General courses:
    • Apply to everyone except the originating department
    • Core courses:
    • Created by departments
    • Electives:
    • Created by departments
    • Required courses:
    • Created by other departments and imposed where needed
    • Departments select from available courses, not recreate them.

⸻

11. GST Classification
    • There are two types of GST courses:
    1.  GSTs created by NUC
    2.  BU GSTs created by VAPOC
        • These courses have:
        • Predetermined application rules
        • Predefined admin and general visibility
        • Required electives can be created centrally, and departments choose from them.
